Calibrate the subtitle match score, and fix what was hiding under it

"52 percent is not good enough" was right about the number and wrong about
the subtitle. Wizdom answers fine - 32 Hebrew subtitles for Shawshank - and
the order was correct the whole time. The scale was not.

The best score reachable from a Hebrew provider was 33: source 15 plus
resolution 12 plus codec 6, against a threshold of 70. Nothing Wizdom
returned could ever be accepted, every film fell through to "below
threshold, used anyway", and every subtitle was labelled a weak guess.

The missing piece was the largest one. Every candidate is the answer to a
search for one specific film, by IMDb id, and that was worth nothing in the
sum - three weak signals added up, the strong one ignored. The scale is now
a calibrated probability, checked against live results:

    100  the same file by hash, or the same release name
     85  the same group, source and codec
     70  the right title, same source and resolution, another group
     55  the right title and the same source
     40  the right title and nothing else
      0  demonstrably the wrong episode

Three real bugs were underneath it.

Two unknown resolutions counted as an agreement. source and codec both
guard against "unknown"; resolution did not, and last night's change to
stop assuming "sd" made it fire far more often.

Every .srt lost its release group, because only video extensions were
stripped - in the one file type where the group matters most.

A language suffix was read as the group: Wizdom returns YIFY-heb, which
parsed as a release by a group called "heb". Subtitle decoration now comes
off first, repeatedly, because it stacks: -GROUP.forced.heb.srt.

That exposed a fourth: with -heb gone, x264.YIFY has a dot-separated group,
which was not recognised at all. It is now, but only on a name already
carrying a resolution, source or codec token - otherwise the last word of
every title becomes a group and The.Office is by Office.
